Sister Edda serves the chapel at the east edge of the square. She
came to Marlow eleven years ago from the chapter in Ostgate and has
not returned to the seat once. She is a grey woman in her sixties
who keeps the chapel clean and the evening service kept until
recently.

She is the only person in the village who can read more than a name
and a price. She reads aloud at funerals, reads the rolls when the
reeve cannot find his spectacles, and reads her own quiet books in
the evening.

She has a brother priest, Brother Wilm, who was sent north on the
chapter's business three months ago. He has not returned. She has
written twice to Ostgate to ask after him. She has had no answer.
This is why she has cancelled the evening service: she has not been
able to bring herself to give it.

She believes in the dragon and she believes in worse things than the
dragon. She does not say what those things are. She prays for the
village and she prays for her brother. She does not pray for
herself; she considers herself too old to ask.
