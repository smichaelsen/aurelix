Drust runs the camp on the forest path. He is a deserter from one of
the southern Crown companies; the company does not know where he
went and does not care to look. He is in his forties, lean, with a
scar across the bridge of his nose and a sword whose guard is
notched from old work.

He keeps three men with him. They are not loyal to him for any
reason but coin. He is loyal to coin and to the small private idea
that if he is paid enough he will leave this country and never see
its rain again.

He does not know who his current employer is. The employer is
called, in writing, "the gold-eyed one." He has been paid in coin
that is unfamiliar to him; he keeps one piece on him for proof in
case the rest does not arrive. He has been told to hold the cargo
and to wait.

The cargo is alive and in a cage. He has been told to keep it
unharmed. He does not like this work but he likes the price.
