Reeve Halden serves the Crown in Marlow's Hollow. He has held the
post for nine years and is on his second wife. He is the closest
thing the village has to law, which means he settles fence disputes
and writes the tax rolls and, lately, posts notices for bounties he
did not expect to need.

He is in his fifties, broad in the shoulders, slow in the knees. He
keeps his sword on a peg above his door and has not drawn it for
trouble in five years. He distrusts strangers from the south on
principle and pays them anyway when they do the work.

He does not believe in the dragon. He believes in the smoke, the
missing travelers, and the silence from the north. He has written
twice to Ostgate for an answer; he has had none. The bounty notice
on the square is what a reeve does when the Crown does not write
back.
