Iskar is a hatchling drake. He is roughly the size of a large cat,
darker than soot, with eyes that catch light the way a coal catches
it before it goes. He has small wings he cannot yet fly with. His
teeth are sharper than his size suggests.

His name is carved on a bone tag tied to the cage he was found in.
The hand that carved the tag was careful. Whoever named him did not
do so casually.

He is young and frightened and unused to people who are not the
ones who put him in the cage. He is wary of strangers and chooses
slowly who to follow. Once he chooses, he follows.

He does not speak. He understands more than he should. He is afraid
of fire that he did not start.

What he is — a drake, or something more — is not known to anyone in
Marlow's Hollow. Edda, who has read more than any villager, would
recognise the shape if she saw it, and would be reluctant to say
what she thinks.
