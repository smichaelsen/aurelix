Toma is eight. He lives with his mother in the cottage behind the
chapel; his father is two summers dead of a fever nobody could name.
He spends his days at the well in the square, partly because his
mother sends him to fetch water and partly because the well is where
the other children gather and where the most interesting people come
through.

He is curious, playful, distractible, and boastful, in roughly that
order. He repeats what he hears from older children as if he saw it
himself. He is honest in the way young children are honest, which
is to say he believes what he says even when it is not true.

His mother has forbidden him from talking about the old tower.
Toma respects this rule about as well as he respects most rules.

He has never been past the second bend of the forest path and he
considers this a fact about other people's bravery, not his own
caution. He carries a stick he calls a sword and has named it.
